# Policy and safety research, 8 Oct 2026

Decision first: **no policy change in 2026 blocks us; the one that bites is the 1 Feb 2027 entry bar, and the standing risk is still "templated / mass-produced".** Safest lessons now: hand washing, tidy up, brushing teeth, getting dressed. Show potty and bedtime with care; do not show bedwetting; show new food, sharing and big feelings only with the rules in section 2.

Evidence labels: **[official]** = YouTube Help page read 8 Oct 2026; **[secondary]** = news/analysis, not an official page; **[not found]** = looked, could not verify. YouTube Help pages show no "last updated" date for most of what I read, so dates below come from the page text or from news reports.

## 2. Lessons: mainstream advice and what the cartoon must not show

Rule for all: no body shaming, no laughing at the one who is struggling, no reward/punishment loops that shame, a grown-up paper hand only for dangerous or private tasks.

**Hand washing** (safest). CDC and AAP: wet, soap, scrub about 20 seconds, rinse, dry; wash before eating, after the toilet, after sneezing, after outdoor play. Plain soap is as good as antibacterial. https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/conditions/prevention/Pages/Hand-Washing-A-Powerful-Antidote-to-Illness.aspx (updated 7 Feb 2022); https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/prevention/about-hand-hygiene-in-schools-and-early-care-and-education-settings.html. **Do not show:** sanitiser as a toy or left in a child's reach (poisoning risk per AAP); a brand soap; showing a count of seconds as spoken words.

**Brushing teeth.** AAP: brush from the first tooth, twice a day (after breakfast, before bed); a grain-of-rice smear under age 3, pea-sized from 3 to 6; spit, do not rinse; children need help until about age 8. https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/Toothbrushing-Tips-for-Young-Children.aspx (updated 14 May 2025). **Do not show:** a big toothpaste blob, swallowing or eating toothpaste, a rinse-after-brushing, a child brushing alone with no helper, brand tubes. Show the grown-up hand dabbing a tiny smear.

**Tidy up.** I found no AAP/Zero to Three page on toddler tidy-up; AAP chores guidance starts at ages 5-7 (https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Chores-and-Responsibility.aspx), and AAP stresses routine and visual cues. Treat this as [not found for age 2-4]; our "every toy has a home" is a routine/habit, not a chore demand. **Do not show:** a child scolded for mess, stacking that could topple onto someone, climbing to reach shelves, small parts left where Pip could swallow them.

**Getting dressed.** NHS children's therapy pages (Cambridgeshire, Bedfordshire & Luton, Devon): let the child try first, start with undressing, give time and praise, practise when there is no time pressure. https://cambspborochildrenshealth.nhs.uk/child-development-and-growing-up/getting-dressed/ **Do not show:** rushing or an adult taking over in frustration; loose cords or a scarf pulled tight; laughing at inside-out clothes. (Crinkle's scarf must never catch or choke.)

**Waiting a turn / sharing.** Zero to Three: true sharing develops around 3.5-4 years, wanting the same toy is normal; adults narrate, validate feelings, use a timer as a neutral rule-keeper, offer "long turns". https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/helping-young-children-with-sharing/ and https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/parallel-play/ **Do not show:** forcing a toy away, anyone labelled selfish, grabbing that wins, hitting or biting, a sad child as the punchline. Frame it as "turns" with a visible timer or sand-glass picture, not "give it up".

**Bedtime routine.** AAP: a quiet, predictable routine at the same time nightly, such as brush, book, bed; avoid active play before bed. https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/bedtime-trouble.aspx (updated 25 Aug 2022); https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/Brush-Book-Bed.aspx (title seen in search, content not fetched). NHS: no screens 30-60 minutes before bed. https://www.nhs.uk/baby/health/sleep-and-young-children/ (seen in search, not fetched). **Do not show:** a screen or glowing device at bedtime, scary dark scenes, loose blankets over a baby's face (no infant sleep), a child left crying or laughed at for fear, bed-hopping or stalling as a reward, candles.

**Trying new food.** AAP: a food may need 10 or more tries; do not pressure, punish, or bribe with treats; serve alongside familiar foods; cut foods small for toddlers. https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/nutrition/Pages/Picky-Eaters.aspx (updated 26 Apr 2018). CDC has a picky-eater page: https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/picky-eaters.html (seen in search, not fetched). **Do not show:** forced bites, bribery with sweets, gagging as comedy, whole grapes/nuts or other choking shapes for Pip, branded foods or a "treat" reward.

**Calming big feelings.** Zero to Three and AAP: stay calm, name the feeling, hold the limit (no hitting), offer comfort or a calm-down space; children lack impulse control until about 3.5-4. https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/toddler-tantrums-101-why-they-happen-and-what-you-can-do/ ; https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/helping-little-people-manage-big-feelings.aspx (seen in search, not fetched). **Do not show:** laughing at the upset one, "stop crying" or ignoring, isolation or time-out as punishment, anyone hitting or throwing something heavy, a big reaction that makes it worse. The comfort is company, a hug, a cozy corner.

**Potty in time** (use care). AAP: readiness shows at 18-24 months, usually trained by 4; keep it positive, child-led; avoid treats, punishment, power struggles and words like "dirty" or "naughty". https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/the-right-age-to-toilet-train.aspx (updated 24 May 2022). Zero to Three: handle accidents without anger; do not pressure or over-celebrate. https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/potty-training-learning-to-the-use-the-toilet/ **Do not show:** nudity or bare bottoms, bodily detail, an accident as shameful or funny, "big kid vs baby" comparisons, any reward chart that implies failure. Show clothes on, closed door or a paper screen, the feeling (a wiggle) and the walk to the potty; keep it inside the "notice the feeling" cue. Have the safety-checker clear it before building.

**Bedwetting** (do not make). NHS: not the child's fault, do not punish, it is common under 5; give water in the day, toilet just before bed, waterproof sheet. https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bedwetting/ (reviewed 11 Apr 2023; next review was due 11 Apr 2026, so the page may have changed). A wordless cartoon cannot carry "this is normal and not your fault" without risking shame. If ever shown, only as "toilet just before bed", never the wet bed.
