# What makes wordless kids' cartoons clear and funny (research 2 of 4, 8 Oct 2026)

Decision: our Ep 11 problem is **not pace**. Measured on frames, our visual change rate (16 changes/min) equals Booba's 86M-view episode (18/min). The gap is what each beat DOES: the cause of the problem is not shown, the first 10 s do not show a predicament, and the middle has 12 s standing-and-smiling stretches. Fix story beats and gags, not cut count.

Method (all read 8 Oct 2026, logged out, region IN): channel Videos/Shorts tabs and video pages read with page scripts (exact view counts); preview frames from YouTube's own storyboard strips (0-60 s of each video, plus sampled frames); a script measured how many of the sampled frame pairs changed a lot ("visual changes/min"). That number counts scene or pose changes, **not** laughs; sampling is every 1-5 s, so treat it as an estimate. Not checked: sound, and gag counts by watching full-length video. Pocoyo has a narrator, so it is excluded from craft findings. Numbers already in `docs/benchmark-oct-2026.md` are not repeated.

## 10 rules we can apply

1. **Open on the predicament, with no intro.** Booba's 86M episode shows the place at 0 s and the hero close-up at 8 s. Booba's new episode spends 30 s on branding, and Oddbods "full episodes" spend about 40 s on a name-card montage. Our rule: the first frame holds the character who has the problem; no logo, no title card before 10 s (put branding in the thumbnail).
2. **Make the first frame the title picture.** Tim & Bubble's title is the problem and the first shots are the dog and baby face; Shaun's "Baby Timmy Is Crying" opens on Timmy; MEOWJI opens on the blue mouth. One image, one problem, readable with sound off.
3. **Show the cause before the effect, with one object.** MEOWJI "Achoo": germ icons (4 s), the tissue (6-8 s), a wrong try (12 s), the right try (20 s). Ep 11's blind reviewers lost the story because the glowing heart had no light source. Rule: for every change, the viewer sees the thing that causes it (a sunbeam, a hand, a ball) on screen first.
4. **Each beat changes the situation; escalate by breaking a rule, not by cutting.** Booba "Cinema Hall": the poster's chest becomes real at 30 s, a pirate steps out at 46 s. Both we and Booba cut about 17 times a minute; his beats escalate. Rule: write the plan as "state A, a thing happens, state B" and delete any 8 s stretch where nothing changes (Ep 11: 72-84 s, 100-112 s).
5. **Land every gag in a face close-up and never hold a static wide shot.** Toby & Milo cut to the sleeping cat's face at 8 s; Tim & Bubble use bath-face close-ups at 12, 16, 52 s; Booba's hero face fills the frame at 8-18 s. Lila's single wide forest shot (16% of frame pairs changed, 725 views) is the opposite. Rule: gag, then 1-2 s on the reacting face.
6. **Build gags from personality plus a prop, and let the baby cause trouble.** Oddbods' best new titles are one character's flaw with a physical predicament: "Rowdy!" 183K, "Newt Tries to Help Fuse... It Gets WORSE!" 168K, "Zee Grows HUGE! Trapped in His Own House!" 160K. In Shaun's eight latest Shorts the top one is the baby crying (53K). Rule: give Pip a trouble-making trait, and each of Snip and Crinkle a flaw that creates the problem.
7. **Show the lesson as an object that changes hands or state, not as a moral beat.** KIDZOKI's 1.75M Short is one juice poured into four cups with faces. MEOWJI shows the wrong try and the right try side by side. Booba sells kindness as a compilation theme ("Good Deeds Day", 2 h, 184K in 7 days) whose episodes are gag adventures. Timmy Time's "I really wish Otus would share" got 8K: a lesson as the title is weak. Rule: lesson = what ends up where; the title never says it. Check with the Lead: the tick/cross marks MEOWJI uses are symbols, not words, but our card allows only numerals and "?".
8. **Episode unit 2-4 min; package the lesson as a themed compilation of 5-8 of them.** Toby & Milo (1K subs) get 5.8K-71K for 3-4 min single-gag stories; Oddbods' newest 21-min uploads have a median of 141K views against 44K for 7:40 and 51K for 2 h (ages 1 day-1 month, so this is a rough guide); Booba and Shaun's top new uploads are 2 h compilations (184K, 314K) with a theme title. Build 5-8 episodes first.
9. **Shorts are one beat, not a compressed story.** Top Shorts are 19-33 s, native vertical, with the problem in frame 0 and 6-7 visual changes in the first 10 s (20-38 changes/min). Larva and Oddbods reuse horizontal clips with captions and get 14-60K, except one 194K outlier; real-person plus cartoon hybrids (MEOWJI, KIDZOKI) reach 100K to 1.7M. Our 43 s pilot Short already cuts fast enough; the problem is that it holds a whole story. Rule: one problem, one change, one payoff in 20-30 s.
10. **Titles name a character and a visible predicament; cadence matters.** Examples: "Bubble Flies Away! 🪁🐶 Kite Day" 42K for a 48-subscriber channel; Oddbods and Shaun "<Character> <situation>! emoji | series suffix". Cadence: Booba daily, Shaun and Timmy daily, Oddbods every ~2 days, Larva every 2-3 days, Toby & Milo every ~3 days (29 videos in about 2 months). One-word titles ("Rowdy!") work only when the thumbnail shows the predicament.

## What to watch out for
- **Market reality for new wordless channels:** in one search for new kids' wordless cartoons (sorted by upload date), 11 of 14 results had under 20 views. Toby & Milo and Tim & Bubble stand out, so their pattern (one clear problem, close-ups, 2-4 min) is worth copying. Both look AI-generated (my estimate from the frames); our handmade paper look is a real difference, and a policy edge under the inauthentic-content rules.
- **Short-form lesson Shorts are won by live-action hybrids**, so a paper-hand arm in frame (our grown-up) is the nearest we can get to a human cue.
- **Confounds:** view counts depend on age and the back catalogue; Booba's 18K for the 9-hour-old episode is "too early", not a failure.

## Apply to the next plan (checklist)
Frame 0 shows the problem; cause visible by 6 s; no stretch over 6 s without a state change; a face close-up after every gag; the lesson is an object transfer; the Short is one beat of 20-30 s.
