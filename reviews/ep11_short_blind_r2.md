# Blind review, round 2 (vertical, 30 s, 1080x1920)

## Beat by beat
- 0-1 s: Night, porch. Bunny draws a "C" on a small pumpkin with a blue crayon. Bear watches with star eyes, reaching in.
- 1.2-2.8 s (close-up): Bear takes the crayon out of Bunny's hand, smiling and eager rather than mean. Bunny's mouth makes an "o".
- 3-4.5 s: Bunny is sad with watery eyes. Bear holds the crayon and Chick looks on.
- 4.5-5 s: Bear notices (his mouth makes an "o") and turns to look at Bunny.
- 5-5.8 s: Bear puts the crayon back in Bunny's hand. Bunny is still sad on the last close-up frame.
- 6-8 s: Wide shot. Everyone smiles with eyes closed. Bunny keeps drawing while Bear looks on, relaxed.
- 9-12 s: Bunny closes the first eye into an "O" and draws a second "O".
- 13 s: Bunny passes the crayon to Chick, who has star eyes.
- 14-16 s: Chick draws a heart nose.
- 17 s: Chick passes the crayon to Bear.
- 18-20 s: Bear sticks his tongue out in concentration and draws a big smile. Face done.
- 21-23 s: Bear draws circles on his own tummy and is very pleased. Nothing visible appears and nobody looks at him.
- 23-25 s: A human arm in a blue sleeve comes down from the top and drops a small LED tea light into the pumpkin. The pumpkin glows. Bunny and Chick get star eyes.
- 26-30 s: Wide shot, then a close-up of Bear still circling his tummy with the crayon, next to the glowing pumpkin. End.

**Lesson:** Grabbing makes a friend sad. Give it back and take turns, and together you make something great.

**First 2 s:** A clear close-up of a grab and a sad face. That is a strong hook.

## Gags
1. 1-5 s, grab, sad face, give back. This is a conflict beat, not a gag. It reads well.
2. 21-30 s, Bear draws on his own tummy instead of the pumpkin. **NOT LANDED.** The set-up is unclear (is he making his tummy a pumpkin? tickling himself?). No mark appears, so there is no break. Nobody reacts. It loops for about 9 s with no topper.
3. 23-25 s, light, glow, star eyes. This is a wow beat and charm, not a gag.

**Charm:** star eyes; Bear's tongue out while he concentrates (18-20 s); the two handovers with smiles (13 s, 17 s); the glow lighting the porch.

The core sharing story is clear and well paced. The last third is weak: an outside hand makes the payoff happen, and the ending gag doesn't read.

## Blocking
- **21-30 s, Bear drawing on his tummy.** This is the final beat and it can't be read: no mark appears, no one reacts, and it lasts about 9 s. A child can't tell what happens or why. It also models drawing on yourself with a crayon, which parents won't love. Either make the result visible (a pumpkin face appears on his tummy) with a reaction from Bunny and Chick, or cut it.
- **23.5-24.5 s, the tea light.** This object causes the payoff but is only about 6% of the frame width, with no close-up. A big arm with no set-up (whose hand?) drops it in. The cause of the glow is easy to miss. Give it a close-up, or have a character bring the light.
- **24-25.5 s, Shorts safe zone.** In the glow shot the pumpkin's right side, including its right eye, sits in the right 12% between 50% and 90% of the height, under the Shorts buttons. Move the pumpkin left.

## Polish
- 5-6 s: Bunny's relief happens across a cut. Hold a close-up beat of Bunny smiling at Bear after the return. Bear's "oops" moment (about 0.5-1 s) could also be held a little longer.
- 0-23 s: A dim purple night wash flattens the colours on a phone screen. Brighten it slightly before the glow so the payoff contrast still works.
- 26-30 s: The glowing pumpkin dips into the bottom 19% on the final close-up. Fine for the ending, but keep it in mind.
- The ending holds on Bear, but the story belongs to all three. A final group reaction to the lit pumpkin would close the loop.
- Audio: -13.5 LUFS integrated, no silent gaps, LRA 5.4. In spec. I only checked levels, not the sound content.
