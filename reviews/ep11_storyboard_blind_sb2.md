# Storyboard blind read (round 2)

## Beats
- 1-5 s: Sunny evening porch. Happy Chick plays with a glowing yellow heart of sunlight and holds it.
- 9 s: Sky turns pink; the heart fizzles into a sparkle in her hand. Chick goes flat-faced.
- 11 s: Dusk, everything purple, heart gone. Chick is worried and sad.
- 14 s: Eyes shut, head down: sad (or sleepy?). One gate heart glows gold.
- 18-24 s: Bear and Bunny arrive. Bear hugs Chick; Bunny holds a notebook. Chick still worried.
- 30 s: Bunny shows her drawing: a pumpkin with a smiling sun face. Plan: make a "sun" pumpkin.
- 44 s: All three draw on a real pumpkin with markers; Chick concentrates, tongue out.
- 62 s: Pumpkin has a smiley face. Everyone is pleased; Chick beams.
- 131 s: Night. The pumpkin starts to glow; Bear and Bunny gasp.
- 144 s: Chick, delighted, lifts a glowing heart-and-smile face. Her light is back.

## Problem by 10 s and its cause
The sun sets, so Chick's sunlight heart fades away. That is only a guess: a child may think she broke or squashed it.

## Feelings
Chick: joy (has the light), then loss and worry (light gone, dark coming), then focus, then delight. Bear: cosy and comforting. Bunny: has the idea and is eager. At 131 s both friends are amazed.

## Lesson
When your light goes out, friends help you make a new one together.

## Other readings possible
- 1 s: three heart glows (in the air, on her belly, on the floor). Is it a toy, sunlight, or her feelings?
- 9 s: the sparkle could read as the heart popping or as Chick squeezing it too hard.
- 14 s: shut eyes could mean sleepy, not sad. The gold gate heart could mean the heart flew into the gate.
- 18 s: the notebook is small; the plan isn't clear until 30 s.
- 62 s: the gate hearts change colour; is that a meter, or just noise?
- 62-131 s: about 70 s with no frames, so I can't check that stretch.
- 131 s: the ceiling lamp competes with the pumpkin as the light source, and "oh" faces can read as fear. What lights it? A candle or carving is unsafe to copy.
- 144 s: the glowing face looks like a lid or cut-out piece. If it was cut, who cut it, and how?
