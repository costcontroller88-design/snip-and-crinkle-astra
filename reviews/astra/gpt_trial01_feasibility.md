# Ep12 recovery candidate — Every Toy Has a Home

**Unmeasured design draft, 20 October 2026. Not approval or a build instruction.**

1. **Rig:** existing walk, reach, gaze, ears and Pip acting support the proposed actions in principle; floor pickup and placement need prototypes.
2. **Props:** reuse playroom designs where recoverable; `episodes/props_playroom.py` returned 404 at the pinned commit, so a finished reusable module is not established.
3. **Duration:** target **240 seconds**, the minimum requested; the added material must earn its time through changing decisions and a second application of the routine.
4. **Evidence:** both gate-1b mismatches and unresolved safety F8 remain in Ep12 history; this candidate closes neither.
5. **Recommendation:** replace the covered-ball search with a visibly obstructed game and putting toys home; commission only a small feasibility proof before a complete storyboard.

## Story card and explicit departures

| Item | Candidate |
|---|---|
| One lesson | When you finish with a toy, put it in its home. |
| Problem | Pip and Crinkle cannot continue their floor-ball game because toys occupy its lane. |
| Visible cause | Snip finishes with a truck and sets it directly in the lane; Pip’s already-moving ball gently reaches it and stops. Ball, truck and Snip’s releasing paw remain visible. |
| Wrong → right | Snip first moves the truck beside another scattered toy, leaving no lasting arrangement; after seeing Crinkle match a toy to its picture basket, Snip returns the truck home. Later, Snip independently puts another finished toy home before restarting play. |
| Outcome | The first cleanup restores shared play; the second application keeps the space usable without another rescue. |
| Pip’s contribution | Pip notices a matching picture and subsequently leads the return of her own toy. She is neither merely sad nor the mistake-maker. |
| Engine/set/music | A; playroom; steel-pan lead proposed, subject to source/variety verification. |
| Departure | Remove the ROADMAP’s buried-ball cause, search and reveal. Retain “every toy has a home,” picture baskets and cooperative tidying. Remove cushion, tow rope, hook, head bucket and concealed teddy walk. |
| Shorts departure | Two required, independently staged Shorts replace the old optional story Short. The smallest package uses a tidy-floor transformation; Diwali decoration is deferred as a separate scope choice. |

The ball is **never lost, hidden, confiscated or guarded**. It stays with Pip or in a clearly visible designated position between play actions. No character reaches into a container, enters one, climbs, throws a toy or walks with obscured feet.

## Causal beat table

**Length: 240 seconds proposed. Lesson mistake: 8–12 seconds.** These are sequence allocations, not permission for long holds. Each sequence needs visible decision/action/result changes within six seconds; otherwise shorten it and report that the duration target is unsupported.

| Time | Meaningful change and causal purpose | Camera contents that must remain whole |
|---|---|---|
| 0–18 | Frame zero establishes ball play, scattered toys and Snip finishing with the truck. Snip places it in the lane; the visible ball stops against it. Pip looks from ball to truck, not accusingly at Snip. | Wide: all three, ball, truck, lane and baskets; close detail retains contact and releasing paw. |
| 18–36 | Snip notices the stopped game, moves the truck beside the other loose toys and checks the lane. The ball can travel only partway because another previously established toy remains. The issue expands from one obstruction to unfinished cleanup. | Medium: Snip, Pip, both obstructions and ball; Crinkle wholly out or wholly in. |
| 36–54 | Crinkle takes an accessible loose toy, compares it with its basket picture and places it inside. The empty floor position remains visible beside the filled home. This supplies an actionable alternative. | Medium/detail: Crinkle, toy, complete basket opening and picture; no cropped head. |
| 54–72 | Snip retrieves the truck from its temporary spot, matches it and puts it home. Pip watches the now-clear spot. First demonstrated correction belongs to the person who caused the problem. | Medium: Snip, truck, picture basket; detail shows release and result. |
| 72–90 | Pip identifies the home of a different toy and returns it. Her contribution establishes that the arrangement is usable by all three. | Pip whole including tuft and feet; toy, picture and basket opening. |
| 90–108 | The friends clear separate reachable work areas. Show differing toy shapes matching pictures, not three repetitions of one identical insertion. Completed areas accumulate visibly. | Wide orientation, then single-worker medium shots with relevant toy/home whole. |
| 108–126 | One larger toy has its own open resting place rather than fitting the small baskets. Snip recognizes that picture and places it there. This resolves an actual remaining object without cramming or stacking. | Snip, large toy and full resting place; Pip watches from clear floor. |
| 126–144 | They inspect the lane from both ends; Crinkle returns the final visible obstruction. Pip sends a gentle test roll. It reaches Snip for the first time. | Wide: both endpoints, uninterrupted ball path, all feet and all three heads. |
| 144–162 | Shared play pays off: each character receives and returns the ball. An established anticipation gag changes the rhythm without another collision. | Wide/medium: ball and receiver’s hands; every on-screen head whole. |
| 162–180 | They choose a short building game. **Before switching**, Pip puts the ball in its pictured home; Snip takes blocks from theirs. A changed activity tests the routine beyond rescuing the original game. | Pip with ball/home, then Snip with block basket; cuts establish continuous object custody. |
| 180–198 | Together they make a low floor arrangement within a separate play area. Pip supplies its final piece. This is active, successful play, not cleanup stretched to fill time. | Wide establishment; medium single-worker views keep pieces and hands whole. |
| 198–216 | Finished, Snip starts toward the ball, notices the blocks still out, pauses and returns to put them home without Crinkle prompting. This is the decisive independent application. | Snip, blocks, pictured home and visible ball position in one medium/wide composition. |
| 216–234 | Pip and Crinkle finish their own areas; Snip gets the ball only after putting the blocks away. Their game resumes immediately on the still-clear lane. | All three, final placements and clear lane; ball never disappears between cuts. |
| 234–240 | A brief character-specific callback and shared acknowledgement finish the result. No prolonged end hold. | All three whole; completed homes and usable floor readable. |

**Pacing risk:** the sorting section, second game and second cleanup could feel repetitive. The second game is justified only if it creates an unmistakable independent choice at 198–216. If that choice reads without the extra activity, do not preserve 240 seconds through slower movement or extra sorting cycles.

## Comedy and sound — design targets, not landed gags

Provisional break targets: **4, 24, 44, 64, 84, 104, 124, 144, 164, 184, 204, 224 and 237 seconds**. Move these around protected lesson actions; do not force a laugh during recognition of the mistake.

Candidate families: Snip’s ears settling a beat after a careful stop (G6); Crinkle discovering his own exaggerated concentration pose (G3); Pip copying two small poses then inventing an unexpectedly different third (G4); a deliberately over-precise inspection ending in a tiny ear/tuft mismatch (G2). Gags must advance or release the current beat. No laughing at Snip’s error; the comic subject signals amusement first.

For each retained gag: visible setup, pause with music resting, visible break, self-directed joke signal, held reaction, cue and payoff sound. **Six variants per gag, final selections, seven-part tests, precise sound map and density verification remain unfinished.** Facial movement alone is not automatically comedy.

## Two self-contained Shorts

**Story Short — 48 seconds.** Own vertical staging using episode events 0–18, 36–72 and 126–144. 0–8: Snip’s truck placement stops the ball. 8–17: Snip notices; Crinkle demonstrates toy-to-picture matching. 17–31: Snip returns the truck; Pip returns another toy. 31–42: clear-lane roll succeeds. 42–48: Pip puts the ball home; callback. Cause, correction and outcome all appear inside this Short.

**Transformation Short — 24 seconds.** Own close camera: 0–4 three scattered toys and their empty pictured homes; 4–9 first placement; 9–14 second; 14–19 third; 19–22 reveal the usable floor; 22–24 character reaction. Every removal and placement has a visible actor and continuous action, with at least one-second results. No magical cleanup. No lamps, loose petals, powder or chalk in the minimum package. Source event candidates: 54–90 and 126–144; re-film, do not crop landscape wides.

## Misreads and stopping conditions

| Risk | Proposed staging response |
|---|---|
| “Snip takes Pip’s ball” | Snip handles the truck; Pip retains visible ball custody until cooperative play begins. |
| “They are sorting colours, not putting finished toys away” | Use recognizable object pictures; show play → finished → home twice. Colour is secondary. |
| “Crinkle scolds Snip” | Crinkle demonstrates on his own toy with neutral brows; no pointing at Snip, frowns or celebratory laughter during correction. |
| “Tidying means never playing” | Restored play is the immediate consequence; the second game is enjoyable and the final game resumes. |

Stop before full production if the placement prototype fails reach/framing, the first 30 seconds cannot show cause continuously, the 240-second animatic needs padding, or either Short loses its causal chain. Preserve the old F1–F8 record; removing an old prop is a proposed mitigation, not a safety closure. Do not reset gate rounds or label this a new first trial. Any subsequent review remains an Ep12 recovery review requiring the owner’s gate decision.
