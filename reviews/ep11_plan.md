## 1. Story card

**Topic:** a gentle Halloween "friendly glow". At sunset, golden hearts of light from the porch railing dance on
the wall and little Pip chases them; then the sun goes down, the hearts fade, and Pip feels unsure in the dimming
porch. Snip draws a plan (a pumpkin with a smiling face that glows). The three friends draw a friendly face on
Crinkle's big pumpkin (round eyes and a big smile from the big two, a heart nose from Pip); Snip asks the grown-up,
the friends stand back while the grown-up's hand cuts the shapes out and puts in a battery tea light (click, no
flame). The pumpkin glows. Pip still glances at the dark wall behind them, so Crinkle turns the pumpkin round to
face it: the little glowing smile lands first on his tummy, and when he steps aside it lands on the wall as a
GIANT friendly face, right where it was darkest. Pip laughs and catches the smile on her wing.
**Lesson in one line:** little ones draw the pumpkin's face and a grown-up does the cutting and adds a battery
light; and when a friend feels unsure in the dark, you comfort them with light, company and a part in the plan.
**Engine:** E (looking after the little one), first use. **Who errs:** nobody; the problem is Pip's feeling.
**Setting:** NEW: a cosy front porch with a garden path, golden sunset turning to early evening (warm oranges,
pinks and purples; never black). **Outcome:** the dark spot that worried Pip becomes a giant smile of light that
the friends made together. **Wow:** real light: the cut smile, lit from inside by a low battery light, throws a
big smiling pattern onto the porch wall (planted at 0 s by the railing hearts, which show that light through a
shape makes that shape). **Music:** playful bouncy organ lead, F major, 116 bpm, swung; Halloween colour from a
tiptoe pluck bass with chromatic passing notes and organ trills, all major (no minor-key spookiness); the
signature jingle (in C) leads into F. **Safety notes:** markers in paws and wing, never a knife; the grown-up's
hand does all cutting with the tool out of view, the friends stand back; a battery tea light switched on with a
click, no candle, no flame, no matches; nobody climbs or goes down the steps in the dark; the light never shines
in anyone's face (only on a tummy, a wing, paws and the wall); no monsters, ghosts, skulls, bats, fangs or
pointy-eyed faces; the face is round eyes, a heart and a smile, and Pip greets the giant face with joy at once.
**Gate 1 additions (safety-checker, 6 Oct; applied by the Lead):** No blade, knife, saw, tool outline, glint or
sawing/stabbing motion is ever drawn, in any shot or Short. The cutting hand stays fully hidden behind the pumpkin;
only the steadying hand and the second sleeve show, and the sleeve makes small quiet taps, not strokes. The "tok"
sounds are soft woody taps no louder than the footstep taps. Snip's flinch stays ears-only. The friends stay at the
bench (Snip x 640, Crinkle x 500, Pip x 560) from 85.0 until the hand has left (98.4); nobody looks at or walks to the
pumpkin before then. The tea light is touched only by the grown-up's hand. It is never shown open, with a battery or
with a compartment; no friend ever holds the tea light or the lid. It goes in, the lid goes on, and it stays out of
reach on the bottom. Its glow is steady (no flicker, no flame shape, no smoke), so it cannot read as a candle.
Source for the safety-checker to verify: AAP HealthyChildren.org "Halloween Safety Tips"
(https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/all-around/Pages/Halloween-Safety-Tips.aspx: small
children draw on pumpkins with markers and parents do the cutting; use a flashlight or battery light instead of a
candle) and "Easing Infants and Toddlers into Halloween Fun" (link in the Halloween benchmark).

## 3. Why every step makes sense

**Set-ups planted from the first shot (0 s):** Crinkle's big plain pumpkin at the front of the porch (he grew it:
pumpkin patch, vines and a watering can in the garden); Snip's sketchbook in paw and the marker box on the bench;
the wind chime hanging from the porch roof just above Snip's ear tips; the right-hand railing with three heart
cut-outs; the sun low over the garden hill on the right; the plain wall above the porch where the giant face will
land; the door on the left (the grown-up's side).

**Cause and effect, in order**
1. The low sun (seen at the right) shines through the heart cut-outs in the railing: golden hearts on the wall.
   Pip steps into one beam and the heart lands on her tummy, then on her wing when she covers it. This teaches
   the rule the wow uses: light through a shape makes that shape on whatever is in the way.
2. The sun sinks behind the hill (seen). As it drops, the hearts slide a little up the wall (real: a lower sun
   sends the light more level) and fade from gold to pink to nothing. The sky turns purple; the porch dims.
3. Pip has lost her light; the porch is dimmer and the wall under the roof is the darkest place. A breeze rings
   the chime and blows leaves (she hears and sees it): she puffs up and hides behind the nearest big friend.
4. Crinkle is polishing his pumpkin with his eyes closed, humming, so he doesn't see her pass (his running joke:
   last to notice). Snip, facing them, sees it and points; the peekaboo follows.
5. Pip shows what's wrong by pointing at the dark wall and hugging herself. Crinkle understands; Snip's ears droop
   in sympathy. Snip sees the shiny pumpkin next to Pip and has the idea, and draws it so we see it: a smiling
   pumpkin with glow lines (the whale Short failed because the idea was never shown).
6. It's Crinkle's pumpkin, so he looks at it before saying yes (happy dance). Snip hands out the markers from the
   box planted on the bench.
7. Drawing: each friend can really reach (checked against the rig): Snip draws the eyes, Crinkle crouches for the
   low smile, Pip reaches the middle with her wing plus the marker. Pip asks with a point ("me?") and the big
   friends make room: looking after her as an action.
8. The drawn pumpkin doesn't glow like the plan (three puzzled tilts), so Snip asks the grown-up by showing the
   plan. Cutting is a grown-up job: the hand gives a thumbs-up and an open palm (wait there), and the friends step
   back to the bench before anything is cut.
9. The hand steadies the pumpkin; the cutting hand works out of view behind it (only its sleeve shows); each drawn
   shape becomes a hole with a soft "tok". The hand lifts the lid by its stem.
10. The hand clicks a battery tea light on above the opening (we see it light: no flame), lowers it in, puts the
    lid back. The holes light up from the top down as the light goes down inside. The light sits on the bottom.
11. It's a battery light, so the friends can safely stand close in its warm light. But Pip still glances back at
    the dark wall: the glow faces the garden, not the wall.
12. Crinkle sees her look, thinks, and turns the pumpkin round to face the dark wall (heavy: the first push
    fails). Because the light sits low inside, its smile is thrown up and back: first onto Crinkle's tummy (he is
    in the way, small and close), then, when he steps aside, onto the wall, much bigger (farther away = bigger,
    as with a torch). Nobody expected it to be so big: everyone gasps.
13. Pip holds her wing in the light and the smile lands on her wing, as the heart did at the start; the wall face
    gets a wing-shaped gap; she pulls away and it is whole. Fear has become a game.
14. The friends paw-bump over the glowing pumpkin (their paws catch some of the light), then crouch to bump with
    Pip too.
