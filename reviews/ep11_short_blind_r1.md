**Lesson:** Don't grab. Wait your turn and pass the crayon on, and everyone gets to help make something better.

**Funny moments:** Bear's smug tongue-out after the grab (about 2 s), Chick's tongue-out concentration (about 14 s), Bear's tongue-out drawing (about 18 s). They are gentle and cute rather than laughs. There is no gag with a setup and payoff.

**First 2 s:** The bear's starry-eyed longing next to Bunny's crayon. The problem (grabbing) lands by 1.5 s, which is strong.

The structure is good: grab, give back, take turns, add a part each, grown-up light, glow. The weak spot is the first 4 s, where the whole conflict and its fix happen too fast and partly out of sight.

## Blocking
1. **0.7-1.5 s, the grab.** Bear's paw covers Bunny's paw and the crayon at the moment of the swap. The crayon is about 6% of the frame width and changes hands in about 0.3 s under the paw. A child sees "the bear now has it" but may not see that he *took* it. Fix: make the snatch a visible tug of about 1.2 s with the crayon unblocked, and add a quick close-up on the two paws.
2. **3.0-4.0 s, the change of heart and the give-back.** Bear's "oh" reaction lasts about 0.5 s, and the crayon goes back in about 0.5 s, again under his paw. This is the moral turning point, but a child may not read *why* Bear gave it back. Fix: hold on Bunny's tear and Bear's guilty face for about 1.5 s, then give the crayon back slowly, held out in the open.

No other blocking issues. Loudness is -14.0 LUFS integrated, with no silent gaps (silencedetect found none at -38 dB). I saw no harsh cuts. The bottom 19% holds only path stones. The right 12% at 50-90% height holds only Bear's idle crayon hand (24-29 s), which is not important. Safety is good: a crayon instead of a knife, an LED instead of a flame, and an adult handles the light.

## Polish
- **5-16 s:** Bear has no "waiting" acting while the others draw. Small business such as paws behind his back, a peek, or a patient nod would make "wait your turn" a visible action, and would make his 17 s turn feel earned.
- **21-24 s:** The giant human hand arrives with no reaction from the characters (neutral faces). A quick look-up and a gasp would sell it as the grown-up helper, not a stranger.
- **24 s:** A pumpkin with a crayon face glows through like a lantern even though nothing was cut out. Kids will accept it, but a small lid opening or a peek of light through the stem would help sense.
- **Comedy:** Only gentle cuteness (about 1 small moment per 10 s, no real gag). One clear physical gag would lift it, for example Bear's smile running too wide, or Chick's heart coming out wobbly and getting a giggle.
- **Look:** The whole first 24 s is very dim with a purple cast, so faces and the blue crayon have low contrast. Lifting the characters a little would keep the glow payoff and improve readability.
- **Framing:** Bear's right eye is cut off by the right edge in many close shots (2-6 s, 18-26 s).
- **Handoffs at 12 s and 17 s** are readable but quick (about 0.5 s). Slowing each pass slightly would echo the lesson.
