**My one-paragraph story:** A bear trips over a toy block and splashes blue paint all over a picture of him and his
bunny friend. He's shocked and guilty, then gets an idea (and a blue nose). Together with the bunny he turns the
splat into a smiling whale, with a spout, a sea, a rainbow and the two friends riding in a little boat. A before/after
wipe shows the change, and they finish with a happy paw-bump.

## (b) Lesson
An accident or mess isn't the end: with a friend you can turn a mistake into something even better. Clear from the
transformation. No "sorry" to the bunny is shown.

## (c) Confusions; too small, fast, hidden or off-screen
- **Faces cut by the frame:** for most of the easel shots (1.75–3.0 and 6.75–15.2, about 10 s) the bunny's face is
  cut in half by the left edge and the bear's by the right edge. The bear's eye and face sit in the right 12%, under
  the Shorts like/comment buttons.
- **The bunny's reaction to the splash is off-frame** (1.75–3.0). I can't tell whose picture it was, or whether
  anyone is upset. The stakes are carried only by the bear.
- **The trip is fast** (0.3–0.7 s) in a medium shot: the block tips and the cup spills in about 0.4 s. It's easy to
  miss *why* the paint flew.
- **The cup turns into a brush** at 3.9–4.0 s with no pick-up.
- **Why does he paint his own nose?** (4.6–5.6) It's funny, but it isn't connected to anything, and nobody reacts to it.
- **The idea cue points at the wrong drawing:** the sparkle lands on the rainbow, not the fish. The main idea
  (splat → sea creature) is never shown as an idea; the rainbow only pays off at 14.8.
- **The bunny looks cross** while drawing (6.75–9.4): her single visible brow slants down. The dark beaded ring is hard
  to read as a drawing outline, and it vanishes at 10.7.
- **Whale or bug?** Spikes on the back and drip "legs" stay on, so until the spout (12.0) it reads as a spiky
  fish or bug.
- **The tiny friends in the boat** (13.2 on) are only about 3% of the frame height, with no close-up. This is the best
  callback in the video, and it's too small on a phone.
- **The end hides the payoff:** in the final wide shot (17.25–18.9) the characters stand in front of the painting.
  The final drift crops the bear at the right edge.
- **The paw-bump is one-sided,** and the brush-to-the-bunny's-face move at 18.4 doesn't land.
