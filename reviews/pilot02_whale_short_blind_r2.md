# Blind review, round 2: pilot02_whale_short.mp4

Reviewer: fresh-eyes-reviewer (new instance). I opened only my brief and the video. The one thing I knew in advance was the file name (it contains "whale"), plus general channel notes from the user's memory index (a paper-puppet kids' channel, and the lessons from pilot 1). I knew nothing about the plot. Because the file name told me to expect a whale, I recognised it sooner than a truly blind child would.

**File:** 1080x1920 vertical, 30 fps, 22.1 s, 16.2 MB, H.264 + AAC stereo 48 kHz.
**Method:** I looked at 1 fps frames at full size, 8 fps contact sheets for the whole video, 10 fps half-size frames of the spill (0.2–1.0 s), and 4 fps crops of the painting (12.4–14.4 s). I measured the audio with ebur128 and silencedetect (-38 dB, 0.25 s) and looked at a spectrogram and a waveform.

**Story as I read it:** the bear trips while carrying paint water and splashes the bunny's picture. The bunny is sad and the bear feels bad. The bear clowns with a blue nose, has an idea, and together they turn the splash into a whale at sea, with themselves in a boat and a rainbow. They celebrate.

## (b) Lesson in one sentence
If you spoil something by accident, help fix it, and a mistake can be turned into something new and even better together.

The "fix it together" half is clear. The "say sorry" half is missing: there is no apologetic gesture to the bunny, and the bear's first action after the accident is to paint his own nose and laugh.

## (c) Where I was confused / what was too small, fast, hidden or off-screen
1. **The blue nose (6.0–6.6 s): why does he do it?** He aims the brush at his nose on purpose. It might be a clown face to cheer the bunny up, but the bunny is not in the shot and never reacts to it. So it plays as a random gag, and it sits exactly where the apology should be.
2. **The idea (8.25 s) is never shown.** Star eyes and a sparkle tell us "idea!", but not *what* the idea is. There is no thought bubble with a whale and no shot of the bear showing the bunny the blob's shape. The next cut shows the **bunny** already outlining, so the handover from the bear's idea to the bunny's work is missing. A child does not know they are making a whale until the spout appears at about 13.9 s.
3. **The painting is too small while it is being transformed (9–17 s).** All the painting happens in a wide two-shot. The canvas is about 43% of the frame width, the painted eye is about 40 px, and the boat with the little figures is about 120×70 px. There is no close-up of the canvas until the wipe at 17.5 s. On a phone, the boat callback and the eye are very hard to spot.
4. **The transformation is too fast.** Outline, eye, tail, spout, sea, boat and rainbow are seven additions in about 8 s. Each new element after the outline appears in roughly 0.3–0.5 s.
5. **The trip is tiny and quick.** The block tilts and the bear flails in about 0.4 s, at small scale. Most children will see "he spilled" but may miss "he tripped on the block".
6. **The bunny's face is cut off at the left edge** in every wide shot (1.75–3.3 s and 9–17.4 s): her face runs off the frame and her left eye is about 30 px from the edge.
7. **The spiky ring around the bunny's head (3.4–4.0 s)** looks like a lion's mane or a sunflower collar rather than a "shock" sign.
8. **The finished creature is still spiky and has "legs".** The splash spikes and the drips under the body are kept, so before the spout it looks more like a spiky fish or bug standing in the sea than a whale.
9. **The painting is hidden at the end.** From 19.5 to 21.4 s the characters stand in front of it. It only comes back in the last 0.6 s.
10. The bear's brush appears from nowhere at the cut at 5.5 s, and the bunting appears in only one shot (1.75–3.3 s). Both are small continuity slips.

## (d) Stretches over 4 s where nothing changes
None over 4 s. The slowest part is **1.75–5.5 s**: a frozen two-shot, then two near-static close-ups, about 3.7 s of very little movement. The audio is also at its quietest here (momentary loudness falls to about -37 LUFS at 2.0 s). That is 17% of the Short in a slow, quiet "sad" section, which is a risk of people scrolling away.

## (e) Funny moments
- 0.3–1.0 s: the bear trips over the block and the water splats onto the painting (slapstick "uh-oh").
- 6.0–6.6 s: the bear paints his own nose blue (the best gag).
- 7.1–8.0 s: a surprised look, then a belly laugh at his blue nose.
- 13.9–14.2 s: the spout appears and both laugh (charming rather than funny).
- Running gag: the blue nose stays to the end (19.5–22 s).

That is about 3 real laughs in 22 s, so the "fewer than one per 20 s" cap does not apply. The trip and the nose are the only strong ones.

## (f) What makes me keep watching in the first 2 s
A cheerful bear walks right past a friend's picture holding a cup of blue water, with a block at his feet. You can feel the "uh-oh" coming, and by 1.0 s the water splats across the picture and he looks shocked. That is a strong, simple hook. The opening frame (0.0 s) is calm, so the hook depends on the first second playing.

## (g) Scary / unsafe to copy / unkind
- Nothing scary.
- Low risk: the bear dabs a paintbrush on his own nose, close to his eyes. A toddler might copy painting their face. This is mild.
- Low risk of seeming unkind: after ruining his friend's picture, the bear laughs at himself before doing anything for her. There is no apology beat. This is not cruel, but the emotional order is off.
